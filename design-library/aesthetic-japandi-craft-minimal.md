# Japandi craft minimal (aesthetic)

**Tag:** material-first warm minimalism

**Canonical references:**
- Isamu Noguchi's Akari light sculptures (1951-) - washi over bamboo ribs, the founding craft object
- The tokonoma alcove and ikebana tradition - one weathered branch, staged in deliberate emptiness (ma)
- Karimoku Case Study / Norm Architects - the contemporary "Japandi" furniture-and-interior register
- Hanko/inkan seal culture - the single vermilion stamp as the only chromatic mark on a quiet page

## Cultural identity

Warm minimalism where MATERIALS are the content: washi ivory grounds with visible fiber, lime-washed plaster, weathered pine, hammered bronze, charcoal ink - swatches of real substance doing the work color blocks do elsewhere. Type is a light humanist or refined serif with wide letterspacing; a vertical CJK micro-column (静間花) hangs in the margin like a scroll inscription; and one vermilion hanko stamp supplies the only saturated mark on the page. The governing principle is ma - negative space as a placed object, not leftover. Beautiful is not the number of things but the interval between them; one branch, three flowers, and the emptiness makes the scene.

Differentiation: `recipe-warm-restraint` is western SaaS warmth - friendly product UI with soft corners and approachable copy, built to reduce friction; Japandi craft minimal is a GALLERY register built to slow you down, and its warmth comes from material honesty (paper tooth, wood grain, bronze patina), not from rounded friendliness. And `style-cream-humanist` is a typography-and-cream STYLE layer; this entry is a whole material world - swatches, stamps, vertical CJK furniture, and ma-driven composition that a type choice alone does not produce.

## Palette anchor

Substrate tones with two quiet accents:
- Washi ivory `oklch(95% 0.01 90)`
- Plaster `oklch(91% 0.01 80)`
- Bamboo tan `oklch(80% 0.05 85)`
- Weathered pine `oklch(50% 0.02 80)`
- Hammered bronze `oklch(30% 0.02 100)`
- Charcoal ink `oklch(22% 0 0)`
- Iris blue `oklch(42% 0.10 262)`
- Vermilion stamp `oklch(56% 0.16 32)`

Vermilion appears exactly once per view (the stamp). Iris is the reserved interactive accent - a deep textile blue, never a tech blue.

## Decoration motifs

- Material swatches as first-class content: washi fiber, plaster, pine grain, bronze macro
- One vermilion hanko stamp - carved-seal glyph, slightly dry-inked - as logo/accent
- Vertical CJK micro-columns in margins; letterspaced romaji sublabels (ICHIBON NO MATSU)
- Single-branch ikebana or one craft object as the only image, held in wide emptiness
- Hairline rules and long horizontal breathing bands; hand-drawn double-curve dividers
- Botanical line drawings (a single iris) in the reserved blue

**Raster required:** the material macro swatches and the craft-object photography (photo `craft-material-macro`, `warm-object-still`). Flat hex fills cannot fake paper tooth or bronze hammer marks; the material honesty is the aesthetic.

## Voice register

Quiet, declarative, sensory: "Paper over bamboo. Light within." "Folded to ship flat. Open to glow." Short sentences that describe making and material. Romanized Japanese used with meaning, never as garnish. No exclamation marks, no urgency, no feature lists - the voice of a maker explaining one object well.

## Failure mode

Flat #F5F5F5 ground with a sans and lots of padding = generic minimal SaaS, not Japandi; without visible material (fiber, grain, patina) the register is absent. Second tell: multiple accent colors or a repeated vermilion - the stamp loses meaning if it appears twice. Third tell: cliché-Japan garnish (sakura, torii, brush-font kanji the designer cannot read) pasted onto a minimal page; the CJK here is structural inscription, not decoration. Fourth tell: crowding - if the empty space stops feeling deliberate, ma has collapsed and the page is just sparse.

## Best for

- Craft, furniture, lighting, and homeware brands; maker portfolios
- Tea, ceramics, stationery, and slow-goods commerce
- Interior architecture studios, gallery and exhibition microsites
- Any brief asking for calm luxury grounded in material rather than gloss
