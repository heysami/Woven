# DeviantArt OC scrawl (aesthetic)

**Tag:** Scene-era amateur character sheet - spiky marker lettering, scribble doodles, toxic purple/lime on graph paper

**Canonical references:**
- Mid-2000s online-art-community "original character" reference sheets - palette swatches, bio stats, adoption rules
- Scene-kid notebook culture: marker on graph paper, spike-edged frames, skull and lightning doodles
- Monster-mascot fan art in the toxic-neon register - purple fur, lime spikes, X-ed eye
- "DO NOT STEAL" watermark culture - ownership panic as page furniture

## Cultural identity

The character sheet a fourteen-year-old made for their original creature at 2am: spiky marker display lettering with jagged black burst-frames, a hand-scrawled bio ("feared and misunderstood, likes loud noises"), palette swatches lovingly labeled, and doodle accents - stars, X marks, lightning bolts, crowns, skulls - scattered wherever a pen could reach. The substrate is purple graph paper; the character art is enthusiastic marker rendering with visible crackle, spray, and scratch textures. A DO NOT STEAL block, adoption rules, and threat-level stat rows complete the furniture.

The defining gesture is **the spike burst**: every title, button, and frame gets jagged marker-drawn edges radiating outward - the amateur's way of making things look intense, applied with total sincerity.

Where `aesthetic-y2k-myspace` is WEB-page chaos (glitter GIFs, autoplay songs, profile-customization sediment), OC scrawl is PAPER - a drawn document, marker and graph grid, fandom bureaucracy like species/mood/diet stat tables. And unlike `aesthetic-kawaii-brutalism` (cute stickers inside a disciplined thick-border container), the scrawl is deliberately UNDISCIPLINED - wobbly hand-drawn frames, scribbled underlines, nothing aligned to a system, polish actively avoided.

## Palette anchor

Toxic clash on a dark paper ground - the swatches are proudly labeled:
- Toxic purple `oklch(55% 0.19 300)`
- Radio lime `oklch(88% 0.25 130)`
- Spike pink `oklch(66% 0.24 2)`
- Electric blue `oklch(76% 0.14 220)`
- Zap yellow `oklch(92% 0.18 100)`
- Bone white (paper panels) `oklch(96% 0.02 95)`
- Void black `oklch(14% 0 0)`

Graph-paper purple is the master substrate; cream panels float on it like taped-on sheets.

## Decoration motifs

- Spiky burst-edged frames and banners (hand-drawn, uneven teeth) around titles and buttons
- Marker display lettering with scratchy strokes; scribble-underline in pink
- Doodle icon set: stars, X marks, lightning, crowns, skulls - drawn, wobbly, multicolor
- Labeled palette swatch rows and stat tables (SPECIES / MOOD / DIET / THREAT) as UI furniture
- Crackle, spray, and scratch texture patches; graph-grid background
- DO NOT STEAL stamp block with curse-threat fine print - the signature ownership furniture

**Raster required:** the character art itself - marker-rendered creature/mascot with crackle and spray texture (raster-foreground on transparent) - plus scratch/spray texture patches. The graph grid and swatch rows are CSS; the creature and the marker strokes are not.

## Voice register

Earnest fandom hyperbole with hand-written energy: "handle with extreme caution!!", "likes loud noises and shiny things", "stealers get cursed". Bio-sheet fragments, ALL CAPS for rules, sincerity always - the humor comes from commitment, never from winking at the reader.

## Failure mode

Cleaning it up kills it: vector-smooth spikes, aligned grids, and a tasteful two-color palette turn it into a brand pretending to be young. Ironic distance ("so bad it's good" framing) reads as mockery - the register only works played straight. Swapping marker lettering for a grunge font, or the creature for a polished mascot illustration, loses the amateur hand that IS the aesthetic. No gradients existed in this world; airbrush glow breaks the marker physics.

## Best for

- Character galleries, adoptables, artist commission pages
- Indie games and webcomics with monster/creature casts
- Community platforms and fan-club spaces celebrating amateur creativity
- Merch and sticker shops in the scene-nostalgia register
