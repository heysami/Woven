# Folk geometric deco (aesthetic)

**Tag:** regional craft modernism

**Canonical references:**
- Seiz Breur movement (Brittany, 1920s-40s) - folk motifs rationalized into a modern design program
- Zakopane Style (Stanisław Witkiewicz) - carved highland ornament as a national design language
- Regional embroidery and carving pattern books - diamonds, chevrons, hooked crosses as a counted grid vocabulary
- Interwar craft-guild specimen plates - alphabet, motif table and application shown as one system
- Nordic and Baltic folk-revival printing - two-color ink on rough stock

## Cultural identity

Folk-geometric modernism: a regional craft tradition - embroidery, weaving, chip-carving - rationalized into an angular type-and-ornament SYSTEM. The letterforms are display capitals built from the same strokes as the ornament (notched terminals, diamond counters, chevron cuts), so type and pattern read as one material. Motifs are catalogued like a grammar: numbered ornament units that combine into borders, badges and navigation marks. The mood is museum-serious and workshop-warm at once - slate and madder red on oatmeal paper, with limestone and woven-textile textures grounding it in real material.

Where `aesthetic-constructivism` is revolutionary and diagonal (agitation, photomontage, shouting red wedges) and `aesthetic-bauhaus` is universalist and reductive (primary colors, geometry scrubbed of place), folk geometric deco is ROOTED: the geometry carries a specific region's memory, ornament is the point rather than the enemy, and the layout stays calm, bordered and symmetric.

## Palette anchor

- Slate `oklch(38% 0.02 230)`
- Stone `oklch(58% 0.01 230)`
- Ash `oklch(76% 0.01 240)`
- Oatmeal `oklch(91% 0.02 90)`
- Madder red `oklch(50% 0.16 27)`

Two inks on paper: slate does the work, madder is the single ceremonial accent (one diamond per motif, the active state, the ritual word). Never a third hue.

## Decoration motifs

- Angular display capitals with notched strokes and diamond counters; a madder diamond dotted into key letters
- Numbered ornament units (diamonds, chevrons, hooked crosses, stepped lozenges) on a counted grid
- Woven border bands framing the page top and bottom, built from repeating units
- Ornament-unit icons instead of a standard icon set - navigation marks drawn in the same grammar
- Limestone, carved-slate and embroidery-weave textures as panel fills, swatch-sized
- Hairline rules with diamond terminals as separators

**Raster required:** the material textures - limestone relief, carved slate, red embroidery weave (the ornament units and letterforms are SVG; the craft materials are not).

## Voice register

Catalog-plate sobriety: "Plate I - Alphabet. Plate II - Ornament grammar." Short declarative labels, small caps, numbered everything. Proud of provenance without tourism kitsch; never folksy-cute, never "handmade with love."

## Failure mode

Sprinkling folk motifs as decoration over a generic sans layout - the system only works when type and ornament share construction. More than two ink colors turns it into a souvenir shop. Rounding the angles or softening the notches drifts it toward friendly-geometric branding; adding diagonals and shouting scale drifts it into `aesthetic-constructivism`. Clip-art "ethnic patterns" from the wrong region is the fatal version.

## Best for

- Cultural heritage institutions, regional museums, folk archives
- Craft breweries, distilleries and food brands with a place-based story
- Festival identities for folk music, craft fairs, regional biennales
- Typeface and pattern foundry showcases
