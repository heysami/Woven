# Constructivism (aesthetic)

**Tag:** aesthetic

**Canonical references:**
- Rodchenko 1924 Lengiz "Books!" poster — the urtext: rotated headline, hailing pose, oxide-red against paper
- Lissitzky *Beat the Whites with the Red Wedge* 1919 — geometric polemic, diagonal as argument
- Rodchenko typeface (revivals by Paratype) — the cap-locked grotesk voice
- Stedelijk Museum identity (Mevis & Van Deursen) — the modern museum-grade descendant
- Ghostly International release covers — constructivist grammar applied to contemporary music

## Cultural identity

Russian avant-garde, 1917–1934 — art conscripted into building a new society. Posters, books, kiosks, theatre sets, agitprop trains. The visual language was forged by Rodchenko, Lissitzky, Stepanova, Klutsis, the Stenberg brothers — practical artists who treated typography and photomontage as engineering, not decoration. The diagonal was political: a refusal of the horizontal complacency of the bourgeois page. Red was the Revolution. Black was ink, was machinery, was print itself. The form survived its political moment and became the lingua franca of museum design, record sleeves, and any publisher whose content has an argument to make.

The mood is **declarative, monumental, mechanical** — never nostalgic, never decorative. A constructivist page is a poster that happens to have a scroll.

## Palette anchor

- **Ink** `#0A0A0A` — never pure `#000`
- **Paper** `#F2EFE7` — newsprint warmth, never pure `#FFF`
- **Oxide red** `#D7261E` — the one accent that matters; never `#FF0000`
- Optional secondary, one only: cadmium yellow `#F2C200` or muted ultramarine `#1B3A8C`
- Caption grey `#6E6E6E` — 50% ink, no other greys

Red is used as a weapon, not as decoration — one block, one wedge, one rule per screen.

## Decoration motifs

**Mandatory vocabulary:**
- One rotated element per screen, angle between 12 and 33 degrees — vary it, never reuse 22 everywhere
- At least one number, date, or word set 3-10x larger than its neighbours
- Photomontage: duotone black + red, hard-masked into geometric shapes (circle, triangle, wedge)
- Page-edge metadata: issue no., date, section, set in mono caps
- Hard-rectangle frames at 2-8px solid ink
- Red rules at 2px used to point at things, not to decorate

**Forbidden tells:**
- Hammer and sickle, red stars, faux-Cyrillic Latin (Я for R, Ж for E)
- Drop shadows, gradients, glass blur, sepia "aged" textures
- Rounded corners anywhere
- Centred layouts
- Serif body type

## Voice register

Declarative, imperative, monumental. **READ.** **BUILD.** **NOW SHOWING.** Numbers and dates are graphic elements, not metadata garnish. Microcopy is verbs and nouns, never adjectives. No exclamation points — the typography is already shouting.

## Failure mode

The AI tell is uniform 22-degree rotations on every diagonal, pure `#FF0000` instead of oxide red, hammer-and-sickle iconography played straight, faux-Cyrillic Latin used as "Russian flavour," and a single rotated hero floating above an otherwise grid-aligned page. Cheap constructivism is costume. Real constructivism commits — the diagonal axis runs through the whole composition, every element snaps to it, and the red earns its place by carrying meaning.

## Best for

- Museum and gallery sites, exhibition microsites
- Record-label catalogues, release pages, festival programs
- Political and activist publishing, manifestos
- Editorial long-reads with an argument
- Film-program and repertory cinema sites
- Type-foundry showcases
- Anywhere the content has a thesis and the layout is allowed to make it loudly
