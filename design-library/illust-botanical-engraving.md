# Copperplate botanical engraving

fine hatched specimen plates with muted natural hand-color on warm cream.

**Distinct from** `illust-beatrix-potter-watercolor`: that style is soft storybook watercolor with narrative animal characters; this is a scientific specimen record, precise engraved line first, color applied thin and secondary. **Distinct from** the `aesthetic-dark-botanical-maximalism` aesthetic: that is lush photoreal darkness and abundance; this is sparse, luminous, one plant isolated on cream with archival calm.

## Visual signatures

- single specimen isolated on cream ground, drawn whole: root, stem, leaf, flower, fruit
- fine copperplate hatching defines every form; color is a thin transparent hand-tint over the line
- muted natural palette: leaf greens, one saturated bloom accent, deep berry darks, graphite grays
- specimen-plate furniture: plate numerals, small-caps Latin labels, hairline rules and baselines

## Prompt keywords

**Primary**: copperplate botanical engraving, specimen plate, herbarium illustration, 18th century natural history

**Material**: cotton paper, cream ground, transparent hand-tint, engraved line

**Line**: fine copperplate hatching, delicate stem contours, hairline detail

**Color**: muted natural greens, single scarlet or berry accent, thin washes over line

**Style**: isolated whole specimen, sequential growth stages, labeled plate composition

**Avoid (negative prompt)**: photoreal, dark moody background, thick paint, cartoon, dense pattern

## Named references

**Illustrators**: Maria Sibylla Merian, Pierre-Joseph Redoute, Elizabeth Blackwell

**Products / films**: Curtis's Botanical Magazine plates, Hortus Eystettensis, apothecary folio prints

## Example prompt template

> copperplate botanical engraving of [CONCEPT], single whole specimen isolated
> on warm cream paper, fine engraved hatching with thin transparent hand-color,
> muted leaf greens and one scarlet bloom accent, small-caps Latin plate label
> and numeral, 18th century natural history folio aesthetic.

## When to use

Botanical and wellness brands, archival editorial spots, apothecary packaging, quiet science storytelling, seasonal journals.

## When NOT to use

loud maximalism, tech dashboards, neon nightlife brands
