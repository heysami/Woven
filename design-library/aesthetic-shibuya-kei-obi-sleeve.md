# Shibuya-kei obi sleeve (aesthetic)

**Tag:** typographic record-shop minimalism

**Canonical references:**
- The obi strip convention of Japanese CD and vinyl packaging - the printed paper band that wraps the sleeve
- Trattoria Records / Cornelius-era sleeve design (Mitsuo Shindo's art direction) - boutique-label typographic play
- Pizzicato Five's catalog-conscious packaging - track lists, credits, and label paraphernalia AS the design
- Tower Records Shibuya shelf culture - sleeves designed to seduce at spine distance

## Cultural identity

The design language of the Japanese record obi, promoted from packaging accessory to the whole aesthetic. One spot ink - classically a hot obi pink - on cream stock, and then TYPE DOES EVERYTHING: a huge vertical kanji title (東京), dense vertical columns of credits and track listings run so tight they become texture, and the humble paraphernalia of the record trade elevated to ornament - catalog numbers (TBP-007), release dates in boxes, price with tax notation (¥2,400 税抜), the compact-disc mark, a barcode, STEREO badges. The composition is rigorous and flat: hairline rules, hard rectangles, one dot-screen halftone panel, silver foil if the budget stretches. It smells of letterpress and record-shop shelving.

Differentiation: `aesthetic-japanese-poster-layout` is photo-dominant - a single dominant photograph carries 80% of the page; the obi sleeve has NO photograph at all - vertical type columns are the image, and the paraphernalia is the decoration. And `aesthetic-avantropop` is glossy avant-fashion pop - rendered, chromatic, image-forward; the obi register is print-flat, single-ink, and typographic to the bone.

## Palette anchor

One ink on cream, at full commitment:
- Obi pink `oklch(58% 0.24 0)`
- Sleeve cream `oklch(96% 0.01 85)`
- Ink black `oklch(17% 0 0)`
- Screen pink (halftone tint) `oklch(75% 0.12 5)`
- Paper gray `oklch(87% 0 0)`
- Foil silver `oklch(80% 0 0)`

The spot ink may be swapped (vermilion, cobalt) but there is only ever ONE, plus black, plus paper.

## Decoration motifs

- Vertical CJK display title with rotated Latin echo running alongside
- Dense vertical type columns (credits, track lists) used as textural field, not for reading
- Catalog-number paraphernalia as ornament: TBP-007 boxes, date stamps, ¥ price with tax note, barcode, disc marks, STEREO badges
- One circular label mark (the "record sticker") repeated as brand dot
- Hairline rules and hard-edged single-ink panels; dot-screen halftone as the only tint
- Washi paper tooth and letterpress deboss; silver foil for one element maximum

## Voice register

Catalog-precise and quietly witty: titles, volume numbers (VOL. 07), formats, credits. The copy IS metadata - 作詞 / 作曲 credit lines, "STREE IN JAPAN", label names. Latin type letterspaced and small. Never exclamatory, never lifestyle-warm; the charm is in the deadpan completeness of the paperwork.

## Failure mode

Adding a photograph collapses it into generic editorial - the discipline is type-as-image. Second tell: a second accent color; two inks plus black is a different (and lesser) genre here. Third tell: the paraphernalia rendered decoratively wrong - a barcode that scans as clip-art, catalog numbers with no system; the furniture must look like it means it. Fourth tell: loose, airy type columns - the vertical columns must be DENSE enough to read as texture from across the room, or the sleeve loses its shelf power.

## Best for

- Music labels, release pages, artist discographies
- Boutique brands wanting print-object credibility (coffee, publishing, stationery)
- Portfolio and studio sites with archival/catalog framing
- Any brief asking for "Japanese minimal" with warmth and wit instead of austerity
