# Toy catalogue primaries (aesthetic)

**Tag:** 80s retail-print maximal

**Canonical references:**
- Argos / Sears Wishbook / Toys "R" Us catalogue spreads, 1980s
- LEGO and Fisher-Price box graphics of the era - unmixed primaries as brand law
- Starburst price splats and "NEW!" flashes of high-street retail print
- Cheap coated catalogue paper with visible newsprint-halftone product photos

## Cultural identity

The page a kid circled with a felt-tip: whole zones of UNMIXED primary - a red block, a yellow block, a blue block, hard-butted with no gradients and no in-betweens - carrying a chubby rounded white display face with exclamation marks it earns honestly ("MEGA TOYS!", "PLAY BIG. PLAY NOW!"). Products live in white photo cards floated on the saturated grounds; every card gets a starburst price splat in yellow with a black price, and a stock code in a plain box ("STK 1723") because this is a CATALOGUE and codes are how you order.

It is commercial earnestness, not irony. The register is cheap print done with total confidence: newsprint halftone in the photos, bold ink coverage that almost smells, and a grid that packs as many products per spread as physically possible.

## Palette anchor

Full-saturation unmixed primaries plus print black and white. No pastels, no tints, no fourth hue.
- Primary red `oklch(63% 0.26 29)`
- Primary yellow `oklch(97% 0.20 110)`
- Primary blue `oklch(45% 0.30 264)`
- Catalogue white `oklch(100% 0 0)` - photo cards and display type
- Ink black `oklch(0% 0 0)` - prices, codes, body

## Decoration motifs

- Starburst price splats (spiky yellow badge, black price) - the signature mark
- Chubby rounded display face, white with soft drop edge, exclamation-ready
- White product-photo cards with squared corners on saturated grounds
- Stock codes in plain keyline boxes; order-form seriousness amid the shouting
- Newsprint halftone dots and bold-ink-print texture
- Hard color-block section changes: red zone, yellow zone, blue zone

**Raster required:** the product photography - studio-lit toys (tin robots, diecast cars, action figures) shot clean on white or on a saturated ground, with a faint halftone print grain. The splats and blocks are CSS; the toys are not.

## Voice register

Exclamatory retail, aimed at the kid and the wallet at once. "PLAY BIG. PLAY NOW!" "ADD TO BAG." "£6.99!" Superlatives welcome, but always attached to a price and a stock code. Never lowercase-cool, never minimalist-hushed.

## Failure mode

Where `aesthetic-neubrutalism` is ART-DIRECTED rawness - offset shadows, deliberate clumsiness, designer irony - this is genuine commercial print with zero self-awareness: no offset-shadow gimmicks, no wobbly borders. And where `aesthetic-pastel-pop-fmcg` softens into milky tints for shelf appeal, this register dies the moment a pastel appears - the primaries must stay unmixed. Muddy secondary colors, gradients, or tasteful whitespace all break the spell.

## Best for

- Toy, game, and hobby commerce; kids' brands with retro parents in mind
- Flash-sale and deals surfaces where price IS the content
- Nostalgic campaign microsites and drop pages
- Any dense product-grid experience that wants joy instead of chill

## Pairs well with

- Shells: `shell-masonry`, `shell-bento-grid`, `shell-mobile-app`
- Styles: `style-bold-display`, `style-flat-design`
- Aesthetic kin: `aesthetic-toy-aisle-window-box` (the constructed-box sibling), `aesthetic-y2k-memphis-loud` (loudness with designer intent, for contrast)
