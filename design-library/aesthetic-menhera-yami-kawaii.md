# Menhera / yami-kawaii (aesthetic)

**Tag:** sick-cute inversion

**Canonical references:**
- Bisuko Ezaki's Menhera-chan (2013-) - the bandaged magical-girl who founded the visual language
- Yami-kawaii Harajuku street fashion and its documentation (the mid-2010s "sick-cute" wave)
- Hospital-set character goods: syringe charms, pill-shaped accessories, eye-patch plushes
- The pastel-goth patch-and-pin economy (safety pins, medical crosses, heart-monitor motifs)

## Cultural identity

Kawaii with a wound showing. The surface is candy pink and mint bubble-type sweetness - a plush bunny mascot, puffy rounded panels, hearts everywhere - but the bunny wears an eye patch, its ear is stitched, it holds a syringe, and the border pattern is a marching row of medical crosses. Menhera (from "mental health-er") culture uses cuteness as a carrier for talking about being unwell; the grimness is never hidden and never dominant - the tension between candy palette and clinical hardware IS the aesthetic. Safety pins, bandages, plasters, and polished-metal hardware sit beside plush fur and glossy vinyl; sweetness and symptom in the same frame.

Differentiation: `aesthetic-positivity-kawaii` is explicitly sunny - its whole contract is uncomplicated joy, and a syringe would break it. Menhera keeps the kawaii toolkit but inverts the message: comfort FOR the hurting, not denial of hurt. `aesthetic-angelcore` handles fragility through heavenly softness - feathers, glow, divinity; menhera is clinical and earthbound - first-aid, not afterlife.

## Palette anchor

Candy pastels with clinical punctuation:
- Candy pink `oklch(78% 0.12 355)`
- Bubble pink `oklch(90% 0.05 350)`
- Mint `oklch(87% 0.09 160)`
- Cotton white `oklch(97% 0.01 340)`
- Ink black `oklch(20% 0 0)`
- Hardware silver `oklch(78% 0.01 260)`

Mint is the "medical" hue - crosses, tape, kit chrome. Black stays in outlines and stitches; never a dark ground.

## Decoration motifs

- Bandaged plush mascot: eye patch, stitches, plaster, held syringe - drawn soft, never gory
- Medical cross as the repeating border/pattern unit (the genre's polka dot)
- Safety pins, patches, and pill/capsule shapes as UI hardware (pins as link icons, capsule chips)
- Bubble display type - puffy, outlined, slightly deflated at the corners
- Dashed "stitch" borders and plaster-strip dividers on cards
- Heart + cross combination marks; drip accents used sparingly

**Raster required:** the bandaged plush mascot art (illustration `menhera-plush-mascot`). The crosses, pins, and stitches survive as SVG; the mascot's plush softness does not.

## Voice register

Sweet with a wince: "Patch me up", "Sweet but tough", "Everything to keep your heart patched." First-person, gentle, a little tired but self-aware. Never clinical-cold, never edgelord-dark, never toxic-positivity chipper - it says "rough day, here's a bandage," not "good vibes only."

## Failure mode

Removing the medical layer leaves plain pastel kawaii - if nothing on screen could sting, it is not menhera. Overdoing it fails the other way: blood, gore, or real-crisis imagery breaks the plush safety contract; the register is symptom-as-symbol, always soft-edged. Third tell: dark-goth grounding - black backgrounds read as pastel goth, a different genre; menhera stays candy-lit. Fourth tell: playing the sickness as a joke; the culture is sincere about feeling bad, and mockery reads instantly.

## Best for

- Mental-wellness apps and communities that refuse corporate cheerfulness
- Character goods, sticker packs, plush and accessory shops
- Zines and fan communities in the Harajuku alt-fashion lane
- Any brief needing cute that acknowledges hurt
