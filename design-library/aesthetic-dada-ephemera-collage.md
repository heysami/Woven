# Dada ephemera collage (aesthetic)

**Tag:** printed-scrap assemblage

**Canonical references:**
- Kurt Schwitters, *Merz* collages (1919-1932) - tram tickets, stamps and newsprint as composition
- Hannah Höch / Raoul Hausmann photomontage pages - the Berlin Dada cut
- Tristan Tzara's ransom typography - mismatched letterpress cases in one line
- Fluxus mail art and rubber-stamp culture - the bureaucratic mark as art material
- Letterpress job-printing ephemera: ticket stubs, luggage tags, fare tables, perforated coupons

## Cultural identity

The page as a drawer of printed ephemera glued down with intent: tram tickets, newsprint scraps, fare stubs, rubber stamps and pointing-hand printer's fists become the actual UI COMPONENTS. A button is a red ticket with a perforated end; a card is a numbered stub with a tear-off edge; an input is a printed form field on aged stock; navigation is a strip of fare-table cells. Type mixes letterpress cases mid-word - roman, italic, wood-type condensed, a stamped word in red - and every element sits slightly rotated, because glue is not a grid.

The differentiator from `style-ransom-glyph-mix` matters: that style is TYPE mixing only - mismatched glyphs on an otherwise ordinary surface. Dada ephemera collage is a whole material world: the mixed type comes WITH its substrate - each word carries its own scrap of paper, its ink texture, its perforation, its serial number. Remove the paper and you have left the aesthetic.

## Palette anchor

Ink on aged stocks - the palette is mostly paper.
- Ticket beige `oklch(87% 0.03 85)`
- Aged tan `oklch(80% 0.04 80)`
- News gray `oklch(76% 0.01 90)`
- Off white `oklch(93% 0.02 90)`
- Letterpress black `oklch(25% 0.01 60)`
- Dull red `oklch(55% 0.14 25)`

One red, always slightly dry and off-register. Never a clean saturated hue; never a white whiter than the palest stock.

## Decoration motifs

- Ticket and stub shapes with perforated edges, torn ends and serial numbers as buttons and cards
- Rubber-stamp frames and overprints (№, PASS, fractions like 2/6) at casual angles
- Pointing-hand printer's fists as the only directional icon
- Mixed letterpress sizes and cases within a single headline; one stamped red word
- Newsprint fragments and mixed paper stocks butted against each other as section fills
- Slight rotations (1-3 degrees) on every pasted element; hard cast shadow the depth of a paper sheet

**Raster required:** the paper stocks themselves - aged ticket textures, torn newsprint edges, stamp-ink bleed (the composition is HTML/CSS; the material is not).

## Voice register

Telegraphic and deadpan-absurd: "ACT GO." "RIDE DIFFERENT." "№ 7." Fragments over sentences; fare-table terseness. Bureaucratic vocabulary (line, zone, fare, stub) deployed with a straight face. Never whimsical-cute, never explaining the joke.

## Failure mode

Clean vector "tickets" with perfect edges and a Google Font = a coupon website, not Merz. Uniform rotation angles and identical drop shadows expose the template. Too much red turns it into circus; zero red makes it beige mush. If every element sits on the same stock the assemblage collapses - the aesthetic lives in the SEAMS between different papers. A tidy 12-column grid underneath defeats the glue.

## Best for

- Music venues, experimental theatres, poetry and zine fairs
- Transit-themed brands, city guides, event ticketing with attitude
- Archives and exhibitions on print culture or the avant-garde
- Portfolio sites for collage artists, printmakers, indie studios
