# NASA archival monochrome

Silver-gelatin space-program photography - rockets on pads, suited figures, mission control - with period grain and documentary framing.

## Visual signatures

- Program-era subjects: launch vehicles, gantries, clean rooms, control consoles, suit fittings
- True b/w silver-gelatin tonality - rich mid-grays, dense blacks, slight highlight bloom
- Documentary framing: wide establishing shots, technicians dwarfed by hardware
- Period artifacts: film grain, registration crosses, frame numbers, archive stamps
- Institutional patience - nothing posed for advertising; everything posed for the record

## Prompt keywords

**Primary**: 1960s NASA archival photograph, Saturn V on launch pad, mission control room, black and white documentary

**Lighting**: available light + period floods; xenon launch glare blooming on film

**Camera / lens**: large-format press camera or Hasselblad, deep focus

**Film stock / post-processing**: Tri-X / Plus-X silver gelatin, archive scan with grain and slight vignette

**Mood**: monumental, procedural, quietly heroic

**Avoid (negative prompt)**: color, digital sharpness, CGI spacecraft, dramatic teal-orange grade, modern hardware

## Named references

**Photographers**: NASA staff photographers (Apollo archive), Ralph Morse (LIFE space coverage), Margaret Bourke-White's industrial lineage

**Archives**: NASA JSC scans, Project Apollo Archive (Flickr), LIFE space issues

## Example prompt template

> An archival black-and-white photograph, 1967: a Saturn V stage suspended from a gantry crane inside the Vehicle Assembly Building, three technicians in white coveralls tiny at its base. Large-format press-camera depth, silver-gelatin tonality with rich mid-grays and dense shadow, fine Tri-X grain, faint frame-number stamp in the border. Available industrial light through high windows. Monumental, procedural, quietly heroic - shot for the record, not for advertising.

## When to use

Monochrome-tech-editorial heroes ("ROCKETS"), aerospace/defense heritage pages, anniversary and history features, atompunk briefs needing real-archive grounding, editorial dossier layouts with FIG-number captions.

## When NOT to use

Current-product launches needing color accuracy, consumer-friendly registers, briefs where "historical" would read as "outdated".
