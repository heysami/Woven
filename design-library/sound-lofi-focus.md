# Lo-fi focus

A dusty looped beat with tape wobble, sitting far enough back that you can work over it.

## Sonic signature

The bed is the register. A slow head-nodding beat somewhere between 70 and 90 bpm, built from a filtered dusty drum loop, a Rhodes or sampled piano chord cycle, and a soft upright bass line. The high end is rolled off hard, there is audible vinyl crackle or tape hiss under everything, and the pitch wobbles very slightly because the source is pretending to be a worn cassette. That wobble is what separates this from generic downtempo; without it the bed sounds clean and modern and the whole reference collapses.

Crucially it is designed not to be listened to. The arrangement has no build, no drop, no vocal hook and no moment that demands attention. The same four bars cycle with small variations, which is exactly the property that makes it work under reading or working. If a listener can identify where in the loop they are, the loop is too eventful.

Interface cues live inside the tape fiction. A soft muted tape stop, a light vinyl tick, a filtered mallet hit, a cassette door clunk for a bigger action. All of them are dulled, none of them are bright, and they should sound like they were sampled off the same worn source as the music. Sharp digital cues on top of a lo-fi bed sound like two different pieces of software running at once.

## Prompt keywords per mode

**SFX**: vinyl crackle, tape hiss, tape stop, cassette clunk, muted mallet, filtered, dusty, warm and dull, wow and flutter, sampled, no bright transients

**TTS delivery**: relaxed, low energy, slightly behind the beat, conversational, quiet, no projection

**Music**: lo-fi hip hop, 74 to 88 bpm, dusty filtered drum loop, Rhodes chords, soft upright bass, vinyl crackle, tape wow and flutter, loops seamlessly, no build, no vocals

## Example prompt templates

**SFX**

> Continuous vinyl surface noise and crackle from a worn record with no music on it, warm and dull with the high end rolled off, seamless 20 second loop.

> A cassette deck tape stop: the pitch and speed dropping away to nothing over about 600ms with tape hiss underneath, dusty and lo-fi, no bright transient.

> A soft muted mallet hit on a filtered Rhodes note, dull and warm, short decay, sampled character with light tape wobble, about 400ms.

**TTS**

> Session started. Twenty five minutes. I will tell you when it is done.

> That is four in a row. Take a proper break, the timer will wait.

**Music**

> Lo-fi hip hop loop at 78 bpm, dusty filtered drum break with a soft kick and brushed snare, mellow Rhodes chord cycle, warm upright bass, vinyl crackle and tape wow and flutter throughout, no vocals, loops seamlessly with no intro, no build and no ending.

## Voice casting

**Antoni** (`ErXwobaYiN019PkySvjV`) is the cast: warm, well rounded and easy to sit under a beat. **Elli** (`MF3mGyEYCl7XYWbV9V6O`) works when the piece wants a younger companion rather than a narrator.

Direct for relaxation and low energy. `stability` around 0.45 so the read has some life, `style` around 0.2, and write conversationally with contractions and short asides. The voice should sit slightly behind the beat rather than on it, which in practice means writing lines that do not need to land on a downbeat and starting clips a beat late in the mix. Duck the bed under the voice but only partially; a lo-fi bed that disappears entirely under a two second line draws more attention than one that just steps back.

## Loudness defaults

- `masterGainBudget`: 0.7
- `fx`: 0.4
- `music`: 0.28
- `voice`: 0.88
- `duckMusicTo`: 0.14
- `duckReleaseMs`: 700

## When to use

Focus timers, study and writing surfaces, personal dashboards, vaporwave and cassette-futurism registers, anything that wants company rather than atmosphere.

## When NOT to use

Serious editorial, memorials, anything where a beat would trivialise the subject.
