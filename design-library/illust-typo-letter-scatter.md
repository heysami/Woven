# Letterform dispersal display

Heavy monochrome display type whose glyphs break loose and scatter - letters dissolve off a word's trailing edge into flocks, streams and weather that literally depict the word's meaning. Type as image: RIVER's letters spill into a winding current of small glyphs, a bird word bursts into a flock.

**Distinct from `style-bold-display`** - that is static heavy type; here the letterforms are mid-transformation, shedding glyph particles that form a picture. Also distinct from `illust-typo-fella-anti-design`, whose chaos is decorative and irreverent; this scatter is semantic - the dispersal IS the illustration of the word.

## Visual signatures

- ultra-heavy grotesque display caps as the anchor voice, tightly set, high contrast against paper white
- the last letters of a word erode into a cloud of tiny scattered glyphs (letters, punctuation, fragments)
- scattered glyphs organize into a depictive shape: a meandering stream, a rising flock, a storm gust, a falling drift
- strictly monochrome or near-monochrome: black ink on off-white, with silver-gray as the only midtone
- per-letter dynamics - letters tilt, shrink and gain tracking as they leave the baseline
- editorial layout discipline around the chaos: clean rules, generous margins, small utilitarian captions
- subtle print grain and ink-mass texture so the dispersal reads as printed matter, not a screen effect
- one diagonal slice or motion cut through an otherwise rigid setting

## Prompt keywords

**Primary**: letterform dispersal, scattering glyphs, heavy grotesque display, type becomes image, kinetic typography still

**Material**: black ink on off-white paper, fine print grain, silver-gray midtone

**Line**: no outlines - solid glyph mass eroding into particle glyphs

**Color**: monochrome black and paper white, one silver-gray accent

**Style**: word's trailing letters break into a flock / stream / gust shaped like the word's meaning, editorial margins and rules keep order

**Avoid (negative prompt)**: color gradients, 3D extrusion, glow, script lettering, decorative random confetti with no meaning

## Named references

**Illustrators**: concrete and calligram tradition (Apollinaire's calligrammes), Ji Lee "Word as Image", lettering-as-picture editorial covers

**Movements**: concrete poetry, semantic typography, Swiss grotesque display culture

## Example prompt template

> Editorial typographic illustration of the word [WORD] in ultra-heavy
> grotesque capitals, black ink on off-white paper, the final letters
> eroding into a scatter of tiny glyphs that form [THE WORD'S MEANING AS A
> SHAPE - a winding stream / rising flock / storm gust], letters tilting and
> shrinking as they leave the baseline, strict monochrome with one
> silver-gray midtone, fine print grain, clean editorial margins and thin
> rules.

## When to use

Magazine features and covers, poetry and language products, word games, publishing brands, title cards that want wit through type alone.

## When NOT to use

dense data UI, long-form reading surfaces, playful candy-color branding
