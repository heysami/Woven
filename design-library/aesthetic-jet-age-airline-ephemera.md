# Jet-age airline ephemera (aesthetic)

**Tag:** 60s carrier print artefacts - carrier navy and jet red on coupon-white stock, engraved crests, carbon numerals, tabbed coupons

**Canonical references:**
- Transatlantic carrier ticket wallets and passenger coupons (Pan Am / BOAC / TWA era) - the paper trail of a crossing
- Engraved airline wing crests and globe roundels - security-printing craft applied to brand marks
- Carbon-copy flight documents: dot-matrix numerals pressed through purple carbon sheets
- Guilloche and check-pattern security papers; baggage stubs, barcodes, VOID overprints
- Diagonal speedstripe livery panels - the jet itself abstracted to red rules on navy

## Cultural identity

The romance of flight carried entirely by its PAPERWORK: carrier navy masthead bands, jet red action color, and coupon-white stock ruled into precise tabular boxes (FLIGHT / DATE / GATE / SEAT, FARE / TAX / TOTAL). Numerals arrive as purple carbon-copy dot-matrix impressions; the crest is engraved line art; backgrounds carry faint security cross-hatch. Everything is a document artifact: tabbed passenger coupons with clipped corners, perforated baggage stubs, VOID stamps, stapled edges. Condensed engraved caps do the display work with institutional confidence.

The defining gesture is **the coupon**: information composed as a detachable ticket - clipped corner, tab edge, ruled fields, carbon serial - so every card and CTA reads as a document you could tear off and keep.

Where `aesthetic-swiss-modernist` shares the era's grid discipline but lives as the POSTER (rational layout as moral position, designer disappearing behind proportion), jet-age ephemera is the DOCUMENT ARTEFACT: its beauty is bureaucratic - carbon impressions, security paper, perforations, the physical residue of a transaction. The grid here is a fare table, not an ethical claim.

## Palette anchor

Institutional trio plus paper tints and one carbon surprise:
- Carrier navy `oklch(26% 0.06 260)`
- Jet red `oklch(55% 0.19 25)`
- Coupon white `oklch(99% 0 0)`
- Ticket blue (tinted stock) `oklch(93% 0.02 250)`
- Stub gray `oklch(89% 0.01 250)`
- Carbon purple (numerals only) `oklch(45% 0.10 300)`

Red is a stamp and an action color, never a background. Carbon purple appears ONLY as impressed numerals and codes.

## Decoration motifs

- Tabbed coupon cards: clipped corners, tab edges, ruled field boxes with small-caps labels
- Engraved wing crest / globe roundel line art as the brand mark
- Carbon-copy dot-matrix numerals for codes, fares, and serials
- Security-pattern paper fills (fine cross-hatch, guilloche) at low contrast on tinted stocks
- Diagonal red speedstripes on navy bands; dashed great-circle route arcs over pale maps
- VOID overprints, perforation dotted rules, staples, and baggage barcodes as functional decoration

**Raster required:** the paper itself - coupon stock fiber, security-weave tint, carbon-copy grain with pressed numerals (raster-photo, document-macro register). The rules, crests, and stripes are SVG; the paper physics are not.

## Voice register

Carrier formality, clipped and gracious: "Thank you for flying Northstar." Field-label diction (PASSENGER, FARE BASIS, CARRIER), verbs of procedure (CHECK IN, VIEW ITINERARY). Never casual, never exclamatory - the calm of an institution that runs on schedule.

## Failure mode

A generic navy website with a red button is not the aesthetic - without coupon furniture (tabs, ruled fields, carbon numerals, perforations) it collapses into corporate default. Full-bleed photography breaks the paper world; imagery enters only as engraved line art or map tints. Rounded-corner cards with soft shadows read as fintech; corners here are clipped, not rounded. Using carbon purple as a brand accent (headers, buttons) ruins its impressed-numeral magic.

## Best for

- Travel booking flows, itinerary and check-in interfaces
- Heritage airline, rail, and hotel brands trading on jet-age glamour
- Event ticketing and membership passes composed as documents
- Fintech or logistics products wanting institutional trust with archival charm
