# Greiman digital new wave (aesthetic)

**Tag:** bitmap-hybrid collage

**Canonical references:**
- April Greiman, *Design Quarterly* #133 "Does It Make Sense?" (1986) - the digitized-body fold-out
- Greiman's Pacific Wave / hybrid imagery era - video stills, MacPaint bitmaps and type layered as one space
- Early Macintosh bitmap tools (MacPaint, 72dpi screens) as a mark-making medium
- Emigre's early bitmap-face issues - low-res type treated as a feature
- West Coast New Wave poster layering - angled baselines, floating fragments, texture as depth

## Cultural identity

The first digital hand: photography that pixel-DISSOLVES mid-image into bitmap fragments, scattered square-pixel debris drifting off a halftone eye, angled duotone type layers slicing across at 8-15 degrees - all printed in a teal-and-magenta pair on newsprint-warm stock. The aesthetic celebrates the seam between continuous photograph and quantized screen: dissolution is the signature move, applied to images, to type edges, to button fills that decay into dither at one end. Layers float in shallow depth, overlapping at angles, but the composition stays airy - fragments in space, not wall-to-wall noise.

Where `illust-typo-weingart-deconstructed` deconstructs TYPE with letterpress-era bars, steps and film collage, Greiman digital new wave is image-led and specifically DIGITAL - the pixel is the brush. And unlike `material-risograph`, whose grain is a print artifact laid uniformly over a layout, here the bitmap texture is compositional: it appears exactly where an image is decaying and nowhere else.

## Palette anchor

A duotone pair plus paper - the two inks do everything, including their halftone mixes.
- Wave teal `oklch(55% 0.10 210)`
- Deep teal `oklch(38% 0.07 210)`
- Hot magenta `oklch(60% 0.24 355)`
- Flare pink `oklch(72% 0.16 0)`
- Pixel mist `oklch(88% 0.04 210)`
- News white `oklch(95% 0.01 90)`

Never a third ink; black only as near-teal. Chroma lives in the magenta.

## Decoration motifs

- Photographs dissolving into square-pixel fragments along one edge; scattered pixel debris
- Bitmap-halftone and coarse-dither texture blocks as material swatches
- Display type on angled baselines (8-15 degrees), layered over and under imagery
- Duotone treatment on ALL photography - teal shadows, paper highlights, magenta accents
- Parallelogram-cut buttons and cards whose fills decay into dither at one end
- Fine registration rules and pt-size margin annotations as visible process marks

**Raster required:** the pixel-dissolving duotone photography - the halftone image decaying into bitmap fragments is generated imagery, not CSS.

## Voice register

Techno-poetic fragments, lowercase-comfortable: "signal to noise." "raster not vector." "does it make sense?" Process vocabulary (scan, dither, stream, index) used lyrically. Curious, not cynical; never corporate feature-speak, never retro-nostalgic winking.

## Failure mode

A CSS pixelation filter slapped uniformly over a stock photo misses the point - the dissolution must be composed, denser at the decaying edge, absent elsewhere. Straightening every baseline turns it into a duotone brochure; adding a third ink color breaks the print logic. Full-bleed noise over everything reads as vaporwave; Greiman's space is airy, with the paper doing half the work. Crisp vector icons floating on the collage expose the fake instantly.

## Best for

- Design schools, museums and retrospectives on digital design history
- Studios and portfolios that want experimental credibility with warmth
- Music and media brands bridging analog and digital identities
- Conference and festival identities for art-tech events
