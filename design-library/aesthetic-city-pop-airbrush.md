# City-pop airbrush sleeve (aesthetic)

**Tag:** sincere 80s Japanese resort-pop

**Canonical references:**
- Hiroshi Nagai - the seaside-pool paintings behind Eiichi Ohtaki's *A Long Vacation* (1981), the founding image of the genre
- Eizin Suzuki - airbrush coastal scenes for Tatsuro Yamashita sleeves (*For You*, 1982), chrome script logotype included
- Niagara / Air Records sleeve catalog - the AOR-era art direction system
- 80s Japanese car and resort advertising (Toyota Soarer, JAL Okinawa campaigns) - the same airbrush optimism sold as lifestyle
- The 2010s YouTube city-pop revival thumbnails - proof the sleeves still carry the whole genre

## Cultural identity

The sleeve art of Japan's bubble-era leisure pop, played straight. An airbrushed sunset runs peach into lilac over a glassy sea; palms stand in silhouette; a distant skyline glitters; and over it all sits a mirror-chrome script logotype with a star-sparkle on the swash. Everything is clean, hopeful, and expensive-feeling - the visual promise of a coastal drive at dusk with the windows down. Katakana title lines sit quietly under the chrome, letterspaced neo-grotesque carries the small text, and the whole frame has the smooth, grainless finish of professional airbrush work.

This is the SINCERE original. `aesthetic-vaporwave` is the ironic remix of exactly this material - it degrades these sleeves with glitch, plunder, and greek busts to comment on consumerism. City-pop airbrush has zero irony and zero degradation: no VHS noise, no pixel smear, no quotation marks. And where `aesthetic-frutiger-aero` is 2000s techno-optimism (glossy OS bubbles, lens flares, aqua plastic), this is analog 80s optimism - airbrush gradient and chrome paint, not gloss and glass.

## Palette anchor

Dusk-gradient pastels with one deep anchor. Analogous harmony, never clashing:
- Peach glow `oklch(88% 0.09 55)`
- Rose haze `oklch(82% 0.10 10)`
- Lilac dusk `oklch(78% 0.10 300)`
- Powder sky `oklch(85% 0.06 250)`
- Aqua sea `oklch(82% 0.09 200)`
- Night drive navy `oklch(25% 0.07 265)`
- Pearl white `oklch(97% 0.01 90)`

The gradient IS the ground: peach at the horizon rising into lilac, never a flat fill.

## Decoration motifs

- Mirror-chrome script logotype with sparkle glints - the signature move, one per composition
- Horizontal airbrush sunset gradient with a glassy sea reflection band
- Palm silhouettes at frame edges; distant skyline as a glitter line on the horizon
- One retro coupe or coastline road when the brief allows a subject
- Katakana title line paired with letterspaced Latin caps ("シティ・ポップ / CITY POP NIGHTS")
- Rounded glassy pills and cards with soft chrome edges; thin hairline dividers
- Neo-grotesque body, generously tracked, always calm

**Raster required:** the airbrushed seascape gradient scene and the chrome script logotype (photo/illustration `airbrush-sunset-seascape`, typography `chrome-mirror-script`). CSS gradients approximate the sky but the chrome lettering and palm-and-sea scene are paint, not markup.

## Voice register

Warm, unhurried, romantic without melancholy. "Midnight cruisin'." "Late night grooves for endless horizons." English loanwords in katakana are period-correct. Never ironic, never internet-damaged, never shouting - the voice of liner notes on a record you saved up for.

## Failure mode

Add VHS scanlines, a greek bust, or a glitched grid and you have made vaporwave - the irony kills the sincerity that defines this register. Second tell: neon magenta-on-black; city-pop is dusk pastel on light, not club neon. Third tell: replacing the chrome script with a plain geometric sans logotype - without the chrome script the genre reads as generic sunset stock.

## Best for

- Music players, playlist apps, artist pages for soul/AOR/city-pop acts
- Travel and coastal-lifestyle brands wanting nostalgic optimism
- Summer product drops, drink brands, retro audio hardware
- Any brief asking for "80s Japan" without the irony
