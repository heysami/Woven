# Typographic theatre

Type performed as dialogue on a paper stage - every voice gets its own color, size and attack. The shouting character is a wall of towering rubber-stamped capitals slammed at an angle; the timid reply shrinks word by word, cascading down the page in perspective until it trails off into scattered characters. Speech made visible through scale, direction and ink pressure alone.

**Distinct from `illust-typo-weingart-deconstructed`** - that style deconstructs type as formal experiment (layers, grids, film collage); this one casts type as CHARACTERS in a scene, with per-voice color and volume. Also distinct from the `aesthetic-zine-type-wall` register, which is an all-over poster wall; this stages a conversation with dramatic empty paper between the voices.

## Visual signatures

- each speaker owns one voice: one color (e.g. red vs blue), one typeface weight, one temper
- loud lines set enormous - condensed caps at a diagonal, filling the width like a shout
- quiet lines cascade in diminishing perspective, words shrinking and tumbling toward silence
- rubber-stamp / letterpress ink distress on the big settings - rough press, uneven ink mass
- stark white uncoated-paper ground; the empty space is the stage
- words bend, taper and arc to act out their delivery (falter, trail off, drown out)
- neutral third voice in small monospace or serif for stage directions and captions
- black is the dominant ink; the two voice colors are the only chroma in the frame

## Prompt keywords

**Primary**: typographic theatre, type as dialogue, expressive typesetting, cascading perspective words, shouting capitals

**Material**: letterpress and rubber-stamp ink on uncoated white paper, rough press distress, halftone screen accents

**Line**: no outlines - solid ink letterforms, uneven stamped edges

**Color**: black ink dominant, one red voice, one blue voice, white paper stage

**Style**: per-voice color and size, diagonal shout walls, diminishing word cascades that trail into scattered glyphs, wide empty staging

**Avoid (negative prompt)**: uniform grid typesetting, gradients, 3D chrome, decorative script, full-bleed photographic backgrounds

## Named references

**Illustrators**: Robert Massin (La Cantatrice Chauve staging of Ionesco), Apollinaire calligramme tradition, futurist parole in liberta

**Movements**: expressive book typography, futurist free-word composition, concrete poetry

## Example prompt template

> Typographic theatre spread staging a dialogue: the line "[LOUD LINE]" set
> as towering condensed rubber-stamped capitals in black with a red accent,
> slammed diagonally across white uncoated paper with rough letterpress
> distress, answered by "[QUIET LINE]" in blue, cascading down in
> diminishing perspective, words shrinking and tumbling until they trail
> into scattered characters, a small neutral monospace caption as stage
> direction, wide empty paper as the stage.

## When to use

Theatre and spoken-word promotion, podcast and audio-drama brands, poetry publishing, conversational products that want typographic drama, expressive landing heroes.

## When NOT to use

calm reading apps, luxury restraint, data-dense dashboards, corporate reassurance
