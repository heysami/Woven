# Cluttercore (aesthetic)

**Tag:** `aesthetic`, `scrapbook`, `maximalist`, `keepsake`

**Canonical references:**
- Sarah Lazarovic's "A Bunch of Pretty Things I Did Not Buy" (2014) - the keepsake-archive impulse made visible.
- Tumblr "junk journal" tag, mid-2010s - pre-aesthetic-flattening era when scrapbooks were genuinely overstuffed.
- Smash Books (K&Company, 2010s) - commercial peak of the keepsake-clutter scrapbook.
- Maira Kalman, "The Principles of Uncertainty" (2007) - ephemera-as-autobiography in published form.
- "Sticker bombing" car/laptop subculture, late-2000s - same density logic in a different substrate.

## Cultural identity

Cluttercore is the aesthetic of the keepsake drawer that overflowed. It is post-Marie-Kondo backlash - a refusal to discard, a belief that joy lives in the accumulation, not the curation. It emerged from millennial nostalgia for pre-digital ephemera (receipts, ticket stubs, photo booth strips) and from junk-journaling and "smash book" scrapbooking subcultures of the 2010s.

The era it conjures is undated on purpose - a scrapbook compiled over years, possibly across generations, with handwriting in many hands. The promise is that every scrap was kept because it mattered to *someone*, and the density itself is the love letter.

It is adjacent to cottagecore (both nostalgic, both maximalist) but distinct: cottagecore romanticizes a place (the cottage), cluttercore romanticizes an *archive* (the drawer, the box under the bed). Where cottagecore is pastoral, cluttercore is domestic-urban - apartment kitchens, dorm bulletin boards, bedside tables.

**Palette anchor** - saturated keepsake mood, unified by the warm scrapbook-board substrate:
- Kraft substrate `#8B6F47`
- Receipt yellow `#FAE099`
- Ticket red `#D54A3F`
- Stamp blue `#2B5A8F`
- Gum-wrapper silver `#C9C7C2`

Every color is fair game so long as the kraft ground holds it down.

## Decoration motifs

The cutout vocabulary IS the aesthetic - 30 to 50 ephemera pieces per page, every layer touching the next:

- Receipts, ticket stubs, fortune-cookie fortunes
- Dried-corsage petals, friendship bracelets, photo-booth strips
- Gum wrappers folded into hearts, mixtape J-cards
- Postcards from places never visited, library-book due-date slips
- Parking tickets, beer-coaster doodles, handwritten phone-number scraps
- Sticker-collection pages, ticket-foil holograms, vintage matchbook covers
- Marginalia in many handwriting styles (suggesting multiple keepers over years)

## Voice register

Diaristic and uncurated. Microcopy reads like a margin scrawl: "kept this," "don't remember why," "from the night we…". Avoid brand-voice polish - captions should feel handwritten, abbreviated, sometimes contradictory. Multiple voices welcome.

## Failure mode

The cheap AI version produces a flat collage of obvious stock objects (a single coffee cup, three Polaroids in a row, one washi-tape strip) on a clean cream background with even spacing. Cluttercore demands overlap, occlusion, and density that registers as overwhelming at first glance - if you can count the cutouts, there aren't enough. The other tell: matching color stories. Real keepsake drawers don't coordinate; they accumulate.

## Best for

- Personal archives, memory apps, journal apps
- Wedding / event recap pages, year-in-review microsites
- Indie zine and small-press product pages
- Fan communities and fandom archives
- Anything where "this was kept because it mattered" is the emotional pitch
