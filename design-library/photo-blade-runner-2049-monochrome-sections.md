# Blade Runner 2049 monochrome-sections (Deakins)

Denis Villeneuve's *Blade Runner 2049* (2017), shot by **Roger Deakins** (Oscar for this film). The signature is the **single-chromatic monochromatic sections** — entire sequences bathed in ONE color (Las Vegas in **deep amber-orange/Martian red**, Niander Wallace's lair in **yellow**, the LAPD orphanage in **blue-grey**, the seawall in **green-grey**). Deakins built **custom Tiffen amber filters** for the Vegas sequences. Soft halogen-like light replaces the original 1982 film's hard noir shadows — there is *"no binary division of light and dark; everyone exists on a continuous spectrum between reality and artificiality."*

## Visual signatures

- **single-color monochromatic sections** — each sequence bathed in ONE dominant hue:
  - **Las Vegas: deep amber-orange / Martian red** (custom Tiffen amber filter)
  - **Niander Wallace's lair: golden yellow** (signifies enlightenment / new plot point)
  - **LAPD orphanage: cool blue-grey** (institutional, melancholic)
  - **Seawall: muted green-grey** (sublime, vast)
- soft halogen-like light source (NOT hard noir shadows — Deakins explicitly avoided the 1982 binary)
- huge scale-of-architecture compositions — subjects dwarfed by vast structures
- atmospheric haze / particulate dust in every frame (depth carrier)
- shallow lateral camera moves; minimal vertical movement
- portraiture stays soft — no harsh fill, faces lit on a continuous tonal spectrum
- 65mm Arri Alexa Plus or Mini, anamorphic 2.39:1 with shallow focus
