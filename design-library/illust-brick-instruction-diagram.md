# Brick build-instruction diagram

toy build-instruction graphics: bold-outlined isometric parts in glossy ABS color on flat instruction-page blue.

**Distinct from** `illust-3d-technical-exploded-precision`: that style is a hairline photoreal device teardown with engineering finesse; this is toy-manual graphics - thick friendly outlines, flat page-blue ground, giant step numerals. **Distinct from** `illust-isometric-tech-saas`: that is soft-gradient tech scenery; this is a literal instruction page with part counts and assembly arrows.

## Visual signatures

- bold-outlined isometric bricks: uniform black keyline around every part, studs drawn as neat ellipse grids
- glossy ABS plastic color: brick red, part yellow, light and medium gray with a single specular edge highlight
- flat instruction-page blue ground with faint plus-mark registration grid at the margins
- giant step numerals dominating the corner of every step panel
- part-count callout boxes: 2x, 1x thumbnails in rounded frames, plus a 1:1 scale check box
- dashed assembly arrows dropping parts into ghosted gray target positions

## Prompt keywords

**Primary**: toy brick build instructions, assembly diagram, isometric construction step, instruction manual page

**Material**: glossy ABS plastic, flat printed page, matte paper blue

**Line**: uniform bold black keyline, dashed assembly arrows, dotted registration marks

**Color**: page blue, brick red, part yellow, light gray, medium gray, ink black

**Style**: numbered step panels, part-count callouts, 1:1 scale tags, ghosted target placement, stud-grid detail

**Avoid (negative prompt)**: photoreal render, hairline technical drawing, painterly, gradient scenery, moody lighting

## Named references

**Illustrators**: LEGO instruction studio tradition

**Products / films**: brick set build-instruction books, part inventory pages

## Example prompt template

> Toy brick build-instruction diagram for [CONCEPT], bold black-outlined
> isometric bricks in glossy red, yellow and gray ABS plastic, flat instruction
> page blue background with faint registration grid, giant step numeral, part
> count callout boxes with 2x and 1x thumbnails, dashed assembly arrows dropping
> a part onto a ghosted gray base.

## When to use

Onboarding and how-it-works steps, feature assembly metaphors, playful product education, empty states that explain a build.

## When NOT to use

moody atmospheric brands, painterly editorial, luxury, organic hand-drawn looks
