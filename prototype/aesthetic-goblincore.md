# Goblincore (aesthetic)

**Tag:** `aesthetic`

**Canonical references:**
- Tumblr goblincore tag c. 2019-2021 — origin of the term, foraging + small-creature reverence
- Cottagecore's "ugly little brother" — the gremlin underside of cottagecore's prettiness
- Victorian natural-history plates (beetles, snails, fungi) — collector's instinct without preciousness
- "Crow brings you a bottle cap" memes — found-object affection
- Forager TikTok / mycology Instagram — Amanita worship, the real-world community

## Cultural identity

Goblincore is the 2019-2021 Tumblr subculture that found beauty in the things cottagecore wouldn't put in its windowsill — moss patches, tarnished spoons, snails, mushrooms (especially the poisonous-looking ones), bottle caps, dirt, frogs. It's queer, anti-aspirational, anti-aesthetic-as-status. Where cottagecore says "I bake sourdough in a sundress," goblincore says "I keep interesting rocks in my pocket."

The defining gesture is the **hoard** — a collection of small found things, loved for their oddness, not their value. Beauty is in the tarnish, the chip, the streak of dirt, the slightly-wrong-looking specimen. A clean, polished thing is suspicious. A thing pulled from under a log is sacred.

The voice is small, affectionate, slightly weird. Latin binomials whispered like spells. The user is a forager, a collector, a small creature themselves.

## Palette anchor

Forest-floor mood, fully desaturated:
- Moss-green `#4A5A3A` — not Material green, never bright
- Mushroom-cap brown `#6B4423`
- Lichen-grey `#8A8870`
- Tarnished-silver `#4F5654`
- Ink-black-brown `#2A2418`

Greens must read as wet, shaded, slightly grey. Any pure saturated green breaks the spell.

## Decoration motifs

The visual vocabulary signals goblincore by what it photographs, not how it draws:
- Real-photo mushrooms — Amanita muscaria (red-and-white), oyster, chanterelle, anything with gills
- Tarnished silverware on velvet — spoons, forks, keys
- Beetle and snail plates from Victorian natural-history books
- Pebbles, quartz clusters, lichen and bark rubbings
- Dried moss patches, found feathers, frog and toad sketches
- Rusted bottle caps, smudged thumbprints, dirt-streaked museum labels
- Handwritten Latin binomials (Amanita muscaria, Helix pomatia) as labels
- Substrate: damp-stained craft paper or dark green velvet
- Typography: smudged-ink handwriting (Architects Daughter or similar) plus stamped Latin

## Voice register

Small, affectionate, mildly conspiratorial. Latin species names treated as endearments. Microcopy speaks to the user as a fellow forager. "Tucked away," "found under," "the small one with the chipped edge." Never aspirational, never wellness-coded. Reverent toward the ugly and the overlooked.

## Failure mode

The cheap version is **clean illustrated mushrooms on a beige background** — flat SVG toadstools, cartoon snails, a serif "forage" wordmark. That reads as Etsy boutique cottagecore, not goblincore. Goblincore requires actual photographic raster of weird specimens against damp substrate, with tarnish and dirt left in. No SVG mushroom has ever been goblincore. If you can't get raster, switch aesthetics rather than fake it.

The second failure is **over-curated hoard** — neatly arranged grid of icons. The hoard is a pile, not a layout.

## Best for

- Foraging / mycology apps, field journals, species logs
- Small personal collections, "interesting things I found" inventories
- Queer-coded cozy personal sites that reject cottagecore prettiness
- Indie shop fronts for weird ceramics, taxidermy, fossil dealers
- Reading lists or zines about ecology, decomposition, small creatures
