# Bauhaus pure (aesthetic)

**Tag:** Bauhaus-Archiv Berlin, Letterform Archive "Bauhaus Typography at 100", Adobe Hidden Treasures of the Bauhaus Dessau, Vitra Design Museum #itsalldesign, Hue Atlas Bauhaus palette

**Canonical references:**

- Adobe Hidden Treasures of the Bauhaus Dessau — revived workshop fonts (Joschmi, Reross) drawn from student sketches.

## Cultural identity

A school, not a style. Weimar → Dessau → Berlin, 1919–1933, then scattered by closure to Black Mountain, Chicago (New Bauhaus / IIT), and Ulm. The reform impulse: unify fine art, craft, and industry; strip ornament; reduce form to its geometric and material truth. Kandinsky's seminar paired primary colours with primary shapes — yellow triangle, red square, blue circle — and that pairing is the movement's secret handshake. Bayer's Universal alphabet abolished capitals as a typographic reform, arguing speech has no uppercase. Moholy-Nagy ran the foundation course on light, transparency, and overlap rather than rendering.

What it signifies today: design as pedagogy, manifesto, civic project. Functionalism with warmth — the cream paper and pigment colours read as humane, not clinical. Reaches for authority through restraint, not loudness.

## Palette anchor

- Studio White `#F5F2E8` — warm cream paper, never `#FFFFFF`.
- Bauhaus Black `#1A1A18` — soft black ink for type and rules.
- Kandinsky Red `#C8302A` — the square.
- Kandinsky Yellow `#E8C018` — the triangle.
- Kandinsky Blue `#1E3878` — the circle.
- Concrete grey `#8A8680` and Workshop Gold `#C89620` as the only supporting neutrals/accents.

Saturation is muted-pigment, never screen-RGB. One primary dominates a composition (~60% chromatic area), one supports (~30%), one accents (~10%); the overall field stays 70/30 neutral-to-chromatic.

## Decoration motifs

- The three primitives — circle, triangle, square — oversized, hard-edged, used compositionally rather than illustratively.
- Kandinsky's colour-to-shape pairing respected (yellow△, red□, blue○) even when broken intentionally.
- Asymmetric balance on an unseen grid; generous white space (≥40% of the viewport).
- Hairline rules and solid black bars separating sections — geometry as punctuation.
- All-lowercase headlines per Bayer's Universal reform.
- 90° rotations of a primitive as the one permitted "movement" — never bounce, never spring.

Forbidden vocabulary: gradients, textures, photographic backgrounds, drop shadows, emoji, dingbats, italics (Bayer rejected them), diagonal "designer" stripes used decoratively.

## Voice register

Declarative. Lowercase. Manifesto-clipped: "form follows function", "build. teach. exhibit.", "art into industry". German compound nouns welcome (Gesamtkunstwerk, Vorkurs, Werkstatt). Marketing adjectives forbidden — no "innovative", no "seamless", no "elevated". Verbs over adjectives. Numbered points over bullet lists.

## Failure mode

Bauhaus 93 or Comfortaa standing in for a real geometric grotesque; screen-RGB `#FF0000 #FFFF00 #0000FF` instead of muted pigment trio; the three primitives floated decoratively on pure `#FFFFFF` with drop shadows; Kandinsky's pairing ignored (blue triangle, yellow square); equal 33/33/33 colour share instead of dominant + support + accent; Title Case headlines in a layout that otherwise committed to Bayer's lowercase; bouncy spring hovers on the primitives. The cheap version mistakes Bauhaus for "primary colours + shapes on white" — it forgets the warm paper, the muted pigment, the pedagogical restraint.

## Best for

Art schools, type foundries, architecture practices, museum and exhibition microsites, design-history publications, manifestos, editorial issue covers, furniture and object brands with a teaching heritage, civic and pedagogical institutions that want authority without ornament.
