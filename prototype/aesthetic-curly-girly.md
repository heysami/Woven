# Curly Girly (Lisa Frank) (aesthetic)

**Tag:** Lisa Frank Trapper Keepers, Club Libby Lu interiors, Bratz 2002 packaging, Claire's c.2002, Curlz MT 1995 / MySpace glitter graphics

**Canonical references:**
- Lisa Frank Trapper Keepers (1990s) — airbrush-rainbow tigers/unicorns/dolphins, glowing white-spot eyes, sticker-stacked density
- Club Libby Lu interiors (2000–2009) — pink-purple-cyan store fit-out, glitter stations, tween "diva makeover" theatre
- Bratz 2002 packaging — magenta/lime/cyan saturation, holographic foil windows, oversized doll-on-pink portraits
- Claire's c.2002 — heart/star/butterfly sticker walls, foil charm cards, fluorescent signage
- Curlz MT (1995) / MySpace glitter graphics (2003–2008) — curled-serif headers, animated sparkle GIFs, heart-dotted i's

## Cultural identity

Curly Girly is the **tween-diva maximalism** of the late-90s / early-00s North American mall — Lisa Frank's airbrushed neon menagerie sliding into Bratz's holographic pop-glam and MySpace's glitter-banner DIY. It is the aesthetic of the sleepover, the Trapper Keeper, the locker decoration kit, the "Princess" birthday party. Saturation is the point. Whitespace is poverty. Every empty zone gets a heart, a star, a butterfly, a glitter speck — not as decoration but as **proof of caring**.

The vibe is pre-irony, pre-restraint: built for nine-to-fourteen-year-old girls before "Millennial pink" sanded the palette down to a single muted rose. Real Curly Girly does not whisper — it squeals.

## Palette anchor

Neon-saturated candy, no pastels. All hues at 90%+ saturation, 50–60% lightness.

- Hot pink `#f30092` — the primary, non-negotiable
- Atomic purple `#ac3cfe` — companion to pink, never grey
- Electric cyan `#05aded` — the airbrush sky
- Nuclear yellow `#fcea08` and lime `#6eb63f` — sparkle pops
- Blazing orange `#f6841b` — warm accent

There are no greys. "Neutral" is white `#ffffff` or candy lavender `#f4e3ff`. Dark text is deep magenta `#3a0a4a`, never black. Holographic foil shimmer (animated multi-stop gradient) is reserved for headings and CTAs.

## Decoration motifs

**Mandatory vocabulary:**
- Airbrushed rainbow-gradient FILL on at least one animal or figure — tiger, unicorn, dolphin, pegasus, butterfly — with a glowing white-spot eye
- Hearts `♥`, stars `★`, butterflies, daisies, glitter specks scattered across every empty zone
- Sparkle GIFs / animated twinkle dots
- Heart-dotted i's in the logo
- Holographic / foil shimmer on at least one surface
- Sticker peel shadow on cards (chunky offset, no blur)
- Curlz MT or equivalent curled-serif on display type

**Forbidden:**
- Memphis squiggles, Y2K-Futurism chrome blobs
- Monoline Lucide / Feather outline icons
- Pastel Millennial-pink palette
- Geometric sans (Inter / Poppins / Geist)
- Whitespace as a value; 1px borders; flat single-stop gradients

## Voice register

Tween-diva exclamatory. "Sparkle Princess!!", "OMG bestie", "you're a STAR". Double exclamation marks, occasional ALL CAPS with stars between★words★, heart-dotted i's in display copy, second-person sleepover register. Never neutral, never institutional, never lowercase-cool.

## Failure mode

AI Curly Girly = Inter on a flat `linear-gradient(pink, cyan)` background, three outline-Lucide sparkles in the corner, a "Comic Sans" h1, and pastel everything. Pastel, restrained, geometric, monoline.

Real Curly Girly is **saturated neon**, airbrush-stippled, motif-stuffed, Curlz-MT-mandatory, sticker-stacked with chunky 4px+ white strokes, holographic accents, animated glitter, and zero whitespace. The cheap version sands the edges off; the real thing piles them on.

## Best for

- Kid / tween products and party-planning apps
- Beauty, glitter, nail, sticker-shop e-commerce
- Fan-club, boy-band, K-pop fandom microsites
- Y2K-revival fashion drops, capsule collections
- Birthday RSVP / e-card / diary / journal apps
- Anything nostalgic for the mall-tween 1998–2004 window

**Avoid for:** anything claiming gravitas — fintech, B2B SaaS, healthcare, legal, "professional services", news-of-record.
