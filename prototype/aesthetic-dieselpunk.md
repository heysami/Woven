# Dieselpunk / Decopunk (aesthetic)

**Tag:** Dieselpunk / Decopunk (BioShock 1–2 Rapture chrome · Sky Captain and the World of Tomorrow title cards · Mafia DE main menu · The Rocketeer / Dark City title sequences · Fallout Vault-Tec signage)

**Canonical references:**
- **BioShock 1–2 Rapture chrome** — the canonical decopunk product: enamel signage, sunburst-and-chevron interior architecture, oxblood + brass + bone palette in a single sealed world.
- **Sky Captain and the World of Tomorrow** — title cards that lock the 1939 World's Fair retrofuture register: stencil, ziggurat, and air-show pulp.
- **Mafia: Definitive Edition main menu** — restrained period typography, period numerals, no shimmer; the "respectful" version.
- **The Rocketeer / Dark City title sequences** — pulp-poster shouting in title-case prose, exclamation marks rationed.
- **Fallout Vault-Tec signage** — the alt-history utility-company voice (`VAULT-TEC` / `WOULD YOU KINDLY`) and the warm-bone enamel substrate.

## Cultural identity

Dieselpunk is the retrofuturism of the **interwar 1920s–1940s**: the World's Fair, the ocean liner, the airship, the prohibition speakeasy, the noir detective, the WPA poster. Where steampunk romanticises Victorian brass-and-steam, dieselpunk romanticises **Art Deco machinery** — the stamped enamel sign, the stencilled rivet, the chrome streamliner, the lobby card. Decopunk is its lighter, more ornamental sibling: same era, more sunbursts and ziggurats, less industrial grime.

The mood is **alternative-history utility-company corporate** — as if Vault-Tec or Rapture Metro or Pan-American Trans-Atlantic Air had a brand manual. Everything is shouted on enamel, framed in bronze, and dated `1947` or `No. 327`. The user is a citizen of a city that almost happened.

This is **not** Edwardian steam, not Renaissance wedding-deco, not Gatsby-gold party-Deco. It is **industrial Deco** — pulp, civic, slightly menacing.

## Palette anchor

- Warm bone parchment `#EFE2C7` — interior chrome substrate.
- Oxidised charcoal `#1B1C20` — exterior signage substrate.
- Warm-black ink `#1F1A14` — never pure `#000`.
- Oxblood `#5A1B26` — danger, CTA, signage fields.
- Bronze `#B08D57` — rails, dividers, glyph strokes.
- Brass highlight `#D4A24A` — active states, the one sunburst.
- Teal-patina `#5E8D8B` — the single cool counter-accent, ~5% of surface.

Greys carry warm hue (60), low chroma (0.012–0.018) — they read as oxidised brass, never neutral.

## Decoration motifs

**One ornament per page, picked once.** The vocabulary:
- **Sunburst / radial fan** — behind the masthead title only.
- **Chevron rule** — section dividers.
- **Stepped ziggurat** — frame for hero panels.
- **Zig-zag lightning bolt** — power / electrical signifier.
- **Rivets** — 4 per panel, corners only, never sprinkled.
- **Period numerals** — `40¢`, `1947`, `No. 327`. Never modern `$0.40`.
- **Enamel double-rule frame** — outer bronze, inner oxblood, bone gap between.

Imagery leans pulp-poster: airships, streamliners, propaganda silhouettes, signage triptychs (`BUY` / `HACK` / `STATS` mirrors the Circus of Values machine).

**Forbidden together:** gears, exposed pipes, steam puffs, brass-fill gradient text, inner-bevel filters, sepia noise overlay, multiple ornaments on one panel.

## Voice register

Imperative pulp-poster. Short. ALL-CAPS for shouting, title-case for prose. Exclamation marks rationed to one per page.

- `WOULD YOU KINDLY`
- `A MAN CHOOSES`
- `POWER TO THE PEOPLE`
- `INCINERATE!`
- `EVE HYPO — 40¢`
- `RAPTURE METRO — LINE 3`

Microcopy reads like enamel signage, not product UX.

## Failure mode

Bebas Neue + sepia noise overlay + gold `#FFD700` brass-gradient text + soft drop shadow on every card + rivets sprinkled like decorative dots + steampunk gears bleeding in = pulp-AI slop. Cinzel or Playfair as the headline face means you've drifted into Renaissance-deco wedding-invite. Pure `#000` background or 8px pill buttons mean you've quit the genre. Multiple ornaments stacked on one panel (sunburst + chevron + ziggurat + rivets all at once) reads as Pinterest-deco, not industrial-deco.

## Best for

- Alt-history aviation and rail brands.
- Prohibition-era cocktail menus.
- Retrofuturist utility companies (gas / electric / water).
- Tabletop / board-game manuals.
- Noir podcast and detective serials.
- Pulp magazine fiction.
- 1930s-set games and films.
- Lobby-card cinema sites.
- Vault / archive / records products with an industrial provenance.
